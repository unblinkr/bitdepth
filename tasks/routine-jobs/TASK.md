## 1. Goal

A one-off measurement, not a nightly family. Before any skill or "cheap lane" product is published, get two numbers per routine job an always-on agent runs:

- what the job costs on a frontier model and on the cheapest endpoints on the route list, and
- whether the job still gets the right answer on those endpoints.

The saving on its own is the price gap restated. The pass rate is the number nobody else publishes.

## 2. Why

Community write-ups for OpenClaw and Hermes Agent name cost as the top complaint and describe manual fixes: move heartbeats and scheduled jobs to a cheap model. One GitHub issue for Hermes puts fixed overhead at about 13,900 tokens a call. Background: `claude/market/agent-community-research-2026-11-13.md` in the claude.ai Project.

## 3. What to build

- `probe/routine.py`: five synthetic jobs generated by code from a seed (heartbeat, monitor, inbox digest, research briefing, ticket tagging), each with an answer a string or number comparison can check; a fixed system prompt of about 13,900 tokens; targets resolved from the route list; a hard spend cap; `run.json`, `responses.jsonl`, `jobs.jsonl`, `system.txt` and `report.md` under `data/routine/<date>/`.
- `.github/workflows/routine-jobs.yml`: manual dispatch only, typed confirmation, estimate step first, refuses while the nightly is running.
- `tests/test_routine.py`.
- `probe/openrouter.py`: `complete` accepts `tag=None` for the unpinned baseline call. Pinned calls are unchanged.

## 4. Rules that apply

- No prompt or answer from a public benchmark. Jobs are generated here.
- Graders compare strings and numbers only. No model output is executed.
- Spend: the lower of the cap input, $5.00, and the nightly cap less the night's billed cost. The run refuses a date with no nightly run record, so the two together stay under $7.75.
- Concurrency 4. Not while the nightly is running.
- The report gives numbers against stated answers and says nothing about why an endpoint answers as it does.
