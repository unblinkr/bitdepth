A unit is a model at a provider-declared precision with a Bitdepth grade, plus the context and latency class it was observed serving. The declared precision and the grade are different attributes: one is a claim held to consistency, the other is a measurement. Anything written on a unit says which of the two it depends on.

Procedure

What runs every night

  1. Probes. 120 fixed prompts, generated once by seeded code and never changed during an experiment, plus 24 prompts generated that night from the same generators with a recorded seed, so they differ every night and can be reproduced later but not predicted. Five families: arithmetic word problems, structured records to JSON, string puzzles, a planted code inside about 2,500 words, and exact repetition of text with diacritics, CJK characters and emoji. No prompt is copied from a public benchmark.
  2. Requests. Every prompt goes to every endpoint of the model on the router, pinned to one provider with fallbacks off, at temperature 0, top_p 1, seed 42, max 512 tokens. A response from a provider other than the pinned one is marked misrouted and excluded. Concurrency is capped at 4 and nightly spend is capped in code.
  3. Reference. For each prompt, the reference output is the one shared by the plurality of endpoints declaring bf16 or fp16. If fewer than two endpoints declare either, the plurality across all endpoints is used and the scorecard says so. A prompt with no plurality is excluded and counted. If the set of reference endpoints changes between nights, the change is dated on the record.
  4. Answer keys. Each family has its own key: the arithmetic result, the JSON values, the string result, the planted code, the exact text. Task accuracy is scored against the key, with no reference to other endpoints.
  5. Self-agreement. Each endpoint is compared with itself on the fixed prompts across consecutive nights (and, on the first night, one hour apart). The share of prompts on which an endpoint disagrees with itself is its noise.
  6. Record. Every raw response, every declared label and every listed price is kept, and each night's folder is hashed and timestamped.
Rubric v0

Four columns, lowest wins, reason printed

One grade per model, endpoint and window (7 nights by default). M: match on the model's stable families. T: task accuracy, as points below the best endpoint for that model in the window. D: declared precision present and unchanged. R: reliability. Latency class and price are shown, not graded. All four column values print with the letter.

How a difference is decided

Separately for each model and each probe family, every endpoint's night-to-night disagreement with itself on the fixed prompts is recorded over the window. The noise floor for that model and family is the 75th percentile of those disagreements across endpoints. An endpoint's match is within noise when it is at or above 100 minus that floor.

The 75th percentile is used rather than the maximum so that one or two highly variable endpoints cannot widen the tolerance for everyone else; an endpoint whose own variation sits above the floor is, by construction, outside noise and reads C or D on M. A family is stable for a model when the median endpoint agrees with itself at or above the stability threshold; M is computed on stable families only, so a family that is noisy for everyone (on night one, arithmetic on one model) does not drag the match column for a model it cannot separate.

A difference smaller than the floor is not reported as a difference, anywhere.

Declared versus measured

Declared precision is what the provider publishes on the router. It is recorded every night and treated as a claim: a grade holds it to consistency over time, not to truth. Measured behavior is what the endpoint returned. Agreement with the plurality is evidence that an endpoint served the same thing the reference endpoints served; it is not proof of the weights, quantization or serving configuration behind it, and Bitdepth does not claim to have verified those.

What a grade does not measure

A grade is not a ranking of models and does not say which endpoint is best. Agreement with the plurality is not the same as correctness, which is why task accuracy is a separate column with its own answer keys. The probe families are narrow by design so that a difference is attributable; they do not cover reasoning depth, coding, tool use or long-task instruction following, and a grade is not a claim about them. The 24 prompts generated each night from a recorded seed limit tuning to the set.

Provisional and final

A grade reads U until seven nights exist for the endpoint. Letters are provisional until the first calibration publishes the thresholds from the first fourteen nights; from then they are final under that version of the method, and the method version prints with every grade. A later version re-grades the archive and says so.

Publication policy

The same rules for every endpoint

  • Every graded endpoint's provider receives its results five business days before publication and may flag factual errors: a misattributed endpoint, a wrong price, a wrong declared label.
  • The review does not change a grade. A verified factual correction is applied and published on the same schedule; it does not restart the five days.
  • No response within five business days means publication as scheduled.
  • Publication then follows the same rules for every endpoint regardless of grade or commercial relationship. A provider cannot choose which of its grades appear.
  • Grades are computed by code from this method. Who pays for a grade, a badge, the feed or a block listing cannot change a letter.

What is published where

  • The Grades board lists endpoints holding A or B, with the window, the floor and the reference rule. Endpoints below B are absent from the board and are not named there. The board is not the complete dataset, and says so.
  • The feed carries every endpoint's grade with its four columns, reason, window, floor and method version, because a buyer asking about one endpoint needs the answer.
  • The archive keeps every nightly input unchanged, with a hashed, timestamped manifest per night.

Numbers against a stated reference. Not a claim about intent. Nothing on this site describes why any provider serves the way it does.
